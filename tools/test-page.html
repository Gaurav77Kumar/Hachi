The Quiet Rise of Boring Technology

A test article · 4 min read

Every few years the industry rediscovers that most software is, in fact, plumbing. The tools that win are rarely the clever ones; they are the ones still running a decade later, maintained by people who valued sleep over sophistication.

ADVERTISEMENT

Consider the spreadsheet. It survived every paradigm shift of the last forty years not by winning arguments but by quietly being enough. The lesson generalizes: durability beats elegance.

When you build something today, the most important question is not what it can do, but who will understand it in five years. Complexity compounds like debt; simplicity compounds like trust.

So the next time a tool promises to be revolutionary, ask it the boring question: will this still exist, unhugged and unfunded, in 2036?
